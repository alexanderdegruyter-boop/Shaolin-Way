# The Eight Pieces of Brocade

The **Ba Duan Jin** (八段錦), the "Eight Pieces of Brocade," is among the oldest and most beloved Qi Gong sets in China — practised for well over **800 years**, and by millions of people every morning to this day. Its name compares the eight movements to pieces of fine, precious silk brocade: a complete, elegant set, each part valuable.

It is gentle enough for any age, asks for no equipment, and takes only ten to fifteen minutes. Yet practised faithfully it loosens the joints, strengthens the legs and spine, massages the internal organs, and calms the mind. You will find a full follow-along version in the **Train → Qi Gong** section of this app.

## The Eight Movements

Each piece has a poetic name and a specific health intention. Move slowly, coordinate each motion with the breath, and never strain.

1. **Two Hands Hold Up the Heavens.** Interlace the fingers and press the palms to the sky, stretching the whole body upward. *Regulates the Triple Burner* and opens the breath.

2. **Draw the Bow to Shoot the Hawk.** In a Horse Stance, mime drawing a bow to either side. *Opens the chest and strengthens the lungs*, and builds the legs.

3. **Separate Heaven and Earth.** One palm presses up, the other down, stretching the torso. *Harmonises the spleen and stomach* — the centre of digestion and energy.

4. **Wise Owl Gazes Backwards.** Turn the head slowly to look behind, first one side then the other. *Relieves the "five strains and seven impairments"* — the wear of stress and overwork — and frees the neck.

5. **Sway the Head and Shake the Tail.** In a wide stance, circle and bend the torso in slow arcs. *Cools excess "heart fire"* and quiets an agitated mind.

6. **Two Hands Hold the Feet.** Fold forward and reach toward the feet, then rise sweeping up the back. *Strengthens the kidneys and the lower back* — the seat of Jing.

7. **Clench Fists and Glare Fiercely.** From a Horse Stance, punch slowly with a fierce, focused gaze. *Builds strength and circulates Qi*, raising vitality and will.

8. **Bouncing on the Toes.** Rise onto the toes and let the heels drop gently, seven times. *Shakes out tension* and is said to dispel "a hundred ailments," settling the whole body at the close.

## How to Practise

- **Breathe with the movement.** Generally inhale as you open or rise, exhale as you close or fold. Let the breath be slow, deep, and quiet.
- **Move within your range.** These are gentle stretches, not contests. Comfort and ease deepen the benefit; force destroys it.
- **Repeat each piece** several times (three to eight) before moving on, or simply follow the video at its pace.
- **Practise daily if you can.** The Brocade rewards the patient far more than the occasional heroic effort. A quiet ten minutes each morning, kept for months, transforms how the body feels.

The Eight Pieces of Brocade is the perfect bridge between breathing and movement — gentler than the workouts, more active than seated breath. Make it a regular companion, and it will repay you for a lifetime.
