# Rooting & the Stances

A tree that stands through the storm is not the one with the thickest trunk, but the one with the deepest roots. In Shaolin training, the legs are the roots, and the **stances** are how we grow them.

## What "Rooting" Means

To be *rooted* is to be connected to the ground so completely that you are difficult to move and yet free to move at will. It is a feeling of weight sinking down through the feet, of the body settled and patient. Rooting is built in stillness — by holding a low stance and breathing — so that it becomes available in motion.

## The Three Foundational Stances

### Horse Stance (Ma Bu)
The king of stances. Feet wide, knees bent, sinking straight down as if sitting on an invisible stool. The back stays upright, the weight even, the breath low. Ma Bu forges the thighs and teaches the body to be heavy and grounded. Begin with twenty or thirty seconds and build slowly toward a minute and beyond.

### Bow Stance (Gong Bu)
A long, forward stance: front knee bent over the ankle, back leg straight and strong, hips squared forward. Gong Bu teaches forward power and stability through length. It is the shape of advancing, of committed forward intention.

### Cat Stance (Xu Bu)
Almost all the weight rests on the back leg; the front foot touches lightly, ready to move. Xu Bu is the stance of alert readiness — light, mobile, and poised. It trains balance and the ability to shift weight instantly.

## How to Practise Stances

- **Sink within your range.** Go only as low as you can hold with good form. Depth comes with months, not minutes.
- **Keep the back upright** and the chest open. Do not let the posture collapse forward.
- **Breathe into the Dan Tian** throughout. Holding the breath turns a strengthening exercise into a strain.
- **Hold to honest fatigue,** then rest. Trembling legs are the forge doing its work — but sharp pain is a signal to ease off.

## Stillness Builds Power

It can feel strange to "train" by standing still. But the stance is where strength, breath, patience, and presence meet. A minute in Horse Stance, breathing slowly while the legs burn and the mind stays calm, is a complete Shaolin practice in miniature.

Build your roots in stillness, and when the time comes to move, you will move from a place that cannot be shaken.
