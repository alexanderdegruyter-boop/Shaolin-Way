# The Breath Is the Root

Of all the things the body does, breathing is the only one that is both **automatic and willed**. It happens without you, yet you can take hold of it whenever you choose. This makes the breath a unique doorway — a bridge between the body you do not control and the mind you are learning to.

## Why Breath Comes First

In the Shaolin tradition, breathing is not a warm-up to the *real* practice. It **is** the practice, the soil from which everything else grows. A strong body driven by a panicked, shallow breath is a powerful engine with no steering. Master the breath first, and movement, strength, and calm all follow more easily.

## High Breath and Low Breath

Place one hand on your chest and one on your belly, and breathe as you normally do. Which hand moves more?

For most people in a hurry, it is the chest. This **high breathing** is shallow and quick — the breath of stress, of fight-or-flight. It fills only the top of the lungs and leaves you subtly anxious and tired.

The Shaolin breath is a **low breath**, drawn down into the belly so that the lower hand rises first and most. This is sometimes called **Dan Tian breathing**, after the energy centre two inches below the navel. Low breathing:

- Fills the lungs more completely, including the rich lower lobes.
- Massages the internal organs with each gentle rise and fall.
- Signals the nervous system that all is well, inviting calm.

## The Four Qualities of Good Breath

Aim for breath that is:

1. **Deep** — reaching the belly, not stuck in the chest.
2. **Slow** — unhurried, never gasping.
3. **Smooth** — even, without jerks or catches.
4. **Silent** — so quiet that someone beside you could not hear it.

Deep, slow, smooth, silent. Return to these four whenever the breath grows ragged.
