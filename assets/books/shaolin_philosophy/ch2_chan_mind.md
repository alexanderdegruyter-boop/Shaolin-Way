# The Chan Mind

*Chan* is the Chinese word for meditation, and the Chan mind is the heart of Shaolin. It is not a special state reached only by saints. It is the ordinary mind, made clear.

## The Still Pond

Imagine a pond on a windless morning. Its surface is perfectly smooth, and it reflects the sky, the trees, and the mountains exactly as they are. Now throw a stone, and the reflection shatters into a thousand ripples. The water has not changed — only its surface has been disturbed.

The mind is this pond. Thoughts, worries, and desires are the stones. **Meditation is not the act of emptying the pond; it is the act of letting the water grow still again.** When the surface settles, you see clearly once more.

## Beginner's Mind

There is a teaching: *in the beginner's mind there are many possibilities; in the expert's mind there are few.* The expert believes he already knows, and so he stops seeing. The beginner looks with fresh eyes and sees what is truly there.

Approach each practice — each breath, each stance, each page — as if for the first time. The thousandth Horse Stance, done with full attention, teaches more than the first done carelessly.

## Presence Over Performance

> "When walking, walk. When eating, eat."

The Chan mind is simply **full presence in what you are doing**. When you breathe, give yourself entirely to the breath. When you train, let the training be the only thing in the world. When you read, read.

This is harder than it sounds. The mind loves to wander — to rehearse the past and worry over the future. Each time you notice it has drifted, you have not failed. *Noticing is the practice.* Gently, without judgement, return. Return a thousand times if you must. Each return is a repetition that strengthens the mind, just as each push-up strengthens the arm.

## A Small Practice

Before your next training session, try this:

1. Sit or stand comfortably and close the eyes.
2. Take three slow breaths, feeling the air move in and out.
3. Notice the sounds around you without naming them.
4. When you feel settled, open the eyes and begin.

One minute of presence transforms the practice that follows. The Chan mind is not built in great leaps, but in ten thousand small returns to *here*, and to *now*.
