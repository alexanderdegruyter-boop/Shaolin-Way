# The Path of Daily Practice

Philosophy that stays in the head is merely decoration. The Shaolin way asks to be *lived*, and it is lived in the shape of an ordinary day.

## Three Threads

A complete practice weaves together three threads, and you will find all three in this app:

1. **Breath** — to settle and energise the mind.
2. **Movement** — to build and free the body.
3. **Study** — to feed and direct the spirit.

On a full day, you touch all three. On a busy day, you touch one. Either way, you keep the thread unbroken.

## A Simple Daily Rhythm

You need not follow this exactly — it is a starting shape you can adapt:

- **Morning:** Three to five minutes of energising breath, then a short warm-up flow. Begin the day rooted and awake.
- **Midday or Evening:** One training session — stances, strength, or flexibility. Move with full attention.
- **Night:** A few minutes of calming breath (the 4-7-8 is ideal), and a page or two of reading before sleep.

Twenty focused minutes a day, kept faithfully, will change you more than two hours done once a month.

## Measuring the Right Things

It is tempting to measure practice in numbers — minutes, reps, streaks. These are useful servants but poor masters. The app tracks them so that *you do not have to*, freeing your attention for the practice itself.

The truer measures are quieter:

- Do you meet difficulty with a steadier mind than before?
- Does the body feel more like a home and less like a burden?
- Are you a little kinder, a little more patient, a little more present?

These cannot be counted, only noticed.

## The Way Has No End

There is no final belt, no summit, no day on which the practice is "finished." The monk who has trained for fifty years still sweeps the courtyard each morning. This is not a failure to arrive; it *is* the arrival. The way is not a destination but a direction — one you choose again with each breath.

> "The journey of a thousand miles begins beneath one's feet."

So begin where you are, with what you have, today. Breathe. Move. Read. And tomorrow, begin again.
